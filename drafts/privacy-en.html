1. Controller and scope

IRIS's operator and controller for the own-business processing described here is [OPERATOR: name or business name, legal form, register, address, NIP, REGON, telephone]. Data protection contact: privacy@irisapp.pl. The Operator has no data protection officer: at the present scale of processing, Article 37 GDPR does not require one.

This notice covers customers and representatives, invitees without accounts, panel and legal-page visitors and people contacting IRIS. We separately describe customer commenters and subscribers. Their controller is the creator or business running the campaign; IRIS acts on its instructions under the version 1 DPA.

This notice covers the panel at panel.irisapp.pl and the IRIS legal pages, which are published today on GitHub Pages. The website at irisapp.pl is a separate project. Before that website starts to collect visitor data other than the data described here, this policy will describe that collection.

2. Sources and whether data is required

Data comes from the account holder, their representative or the operator adding an invitation; Meta's official API and webhooks; Stripe and Resend for payments and delivery; forms, browser requests and correspondence. Connecting Instagram supplies account identifiers, username, account type, permissions and token. We do not request Instagram passwords or scrape.

Providing data needed for sign-in, the account and a selected service is voluntary but necessary to provide that service. Statutory billing data is needed for proper accounting. A request should contain enough information to respond or identify the matter; please avoid unnecessary data.

4. Customer commenters, message senders and subscribers

When a comment enters a campaign, IRIS may create a contact with Instagram ID and username before an email address or marketing consent exists. We handle comments, raw webhooks with comments/DMs, private/public replies, capture forms, six-digit codes and digests, email addresses, confirmations, lists, tags, consent, unsubscribes, suppressions, delivery events and daily link-click counts that do not say who clicked. Identifiers can also appear in job logs.

Instagram sends IRIS every private message of the connected account. IRIS stores the raw delivery for 90 days, but acts only on messages in a conversation that a campaign started. IRIS records list membership when a person gives the address, before the address is confirmed. Only a confirmed address with marketing consent receives a broadcast.

The creator determines purpose and basis, provides its notice and is responsible for lawful communications. IRIS does not replace that notice. IRIS has no field for the creator's privacy notice today. For this reason, capture pages and the footers of emails sent for the creator link today to this IRIS privacy policy, not to the creator's notice. The creator must give its own notice to these people. A comment or use of IRIS is not consent to unrestricted marketing. Resource delivery and later marketing are distinct purposes. The creator can use the privacy notice template for creators. A consent record in IRIS alone does not prove that the consent is valid; the creator assesses that. IRIS does not check the age of commenters or subscribers.

Consent records contain scope, channel, time, key, recorded wording and language. The DM path reads wording from the sent private reply; if none is recorded it stores wording rendered at capture for delivery, without marketing consent. Assessment of this fallback and historical records remains open in the DPA.

6. Transfers outside the EEA

A database in France does not mean that all data stays in the EEA. Data goes outside the EEA in these cases:

  • Resend: IRIS sends mail from the AWS region eu-west-1 (Ireland), but Resend does its main processing in the USA, including account data and metadata. The basis is the EU Standard Contractual Clauses (Commission Decision 2021/914, Module 2, and Module 3 for data that the customer entrusts) in the Resend data processing agreement. Resend also states that it complies with the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework.
  • Cloudflare operates globally, and traffic can pass through servers outside the EEA. Cloudflare states that it complies with the Data Privacy Framework; where that framework does not cover a transfer, the Cloudflare data processing agreement applies the EU Standard Contractual Clauses (Module 2 or 3).
  • Stripe transfers data to Stripe, LLC in the USA. The basis is the EU Standard Contractual Clauses in the Stripe data processing agreement, and Stripe, LLC states that it complies with the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework.
  • GitHub processes visit data in the USA. GitHub states that it complies with the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework and uses the EU Standard Contractual Clauses.

The OVHcloud server is in France (EEA), and OVHcloud states that it keeps the mailbox in Europe. Meta Platforms Ireland Ltd. is responsible for transfers on its platform as an independent controller.

The contact in section 1 provides information and access to copies of applicable safeguards, protecting confidentiality and other people's rights. Policy acceptance does not replace a transfer basis or marketing consent.

7. Disconnecting, deauthorization and account deletion

Disconnecting the Instagram account in IRIS deletes the connection, the encrypted token and the technical records of that account: received notifications, the message queue, automation executions, and the history of Meta calls and connection states. It also pauses the campaigns of that account. It does not delete the IRIS account, contacts, conversations, consents, campaigns, templates or billing.

Removing IRIS in the Instagram settings deletes the token, but not the other data stored in IRIS. Meta then tells IRIS, and IRIS marks the connection as disabled and stops sending from it and reading its comments. In the same operation IRIS deletes the encrypted access token of that connection from the database. Contacts and campaigns remain; reconnecting allows service to resume. To delete the other data, the customer disconnects the account in IRIS, deletes the IRIS account or sends a data deletion request through Meta. A deletion request through Meta deletes the connection to the named Instagram account, the token and the technical records, as a disconnection does; it does not delete contacts, campaigns or the IRIS account. The Data deletion page gives the details.

IRIS account deletion is separate. An export is prepared first and available for 24 hours; downloading it is not a deletion condition. The sequence ends connections/services and purges account data. The DPA provides 30 days with the mail-event/export/proof exceptions above. Own billing data required by law may remain longer. The deletion runs in steps, and IRIS records each step that is done. After an error, IRIS repeats the deletion from the recorded step. When the retries run out, the account deletion screen shows the error and lets you start the deletion again; you can also write to privacy@irisapp.pl.

When Meta does not answer, or IRIS's own call limit is used up, the disconnection still happens at once, and IRIS sends the request to switch off the notifications at Meta later. Until then a queued job holds the Instagram account identifier and the encrypted token, also after the IRIS account is deleted. IRIS usually repeats the attempt for some hours: up to 8 attempts, 5 minutes to one hour apart. When a switch or a protection on the IRIS side holds calls back, the job waits until it is released. IRIS deletes the record of the job not later than 7 days after the job ends: 24 hours after it completes, or 7 days after it is discarded. The record of the disconnected account (Instagram identifier and dates) stays until the notifications are switched off and for 90 days after that.

IRIS makes no backups of the database today, thus deleted data does not stay in backups. When backups exist, this policy will give their retention period. Images can stay in the caches of Cloudflare and of browsers for up to a year, because the cache header allows that long; IRIS does not delete these copies. The Operator deletes mailbox messages by hand after the periods in section 3, and logs go when newer logs overwrite them. Providers delete data in their own systems under their own rules.

8. Cookies, browser storage and click measurement

The cookie and browser-storage notice lists names, purposes and periods. The reviewed application code contains no external analytics or advertising tracking scripts. This does not exclude provider logs or email click measurement.

IRIS counts email-link clicks per link and per UTC day: link, day and number of clicks that day, without naming the recipient or the send, and without IP or user agent in the count. Resend open/click tracking is disabled; IRIS redirects still count clicks. This applies to the links in broadcasts to contacts and in the email with the requested resource. The link goes first to an IRIS address with the path /r/, and IRIS redirects at once to the target address. IRIS does not measure email opens. It does not count a visit that looks automated (for example a mail scanner or a link preview), or visits past the limit of 120 counted clicks per minute from one IP address; the redirect always works.

The link in a message carries the link identifier alone, so IRIS does not assign the click to a contact or a send. IRIS uses the clicker's IP address (an IPv6 address shortened to /64) and browser user agent in process memory only: the IP address for the limit of counted clicks, and the user agent to leave out robots. It does not write them to the database or to logs. The measurement uses no cookies. IRIS processes click data on the instructions of the creator, who is its controller and decides the legal basis (section 4). Click counts are deleted with the link (also with the broadcast it belongs to) or with the customer account; person erasure does not cover them.

9. Rights and contact

Subject to GDPR conditions, you may request access, rectification, erasure, restriction and portability. You may object to legitimate-interest processing. Where consent is the basis, you may withdraw it without affecting earlier lawfulness. You may complain to the Polish supervisory authority, Prezes Urzędu Ochrony Danych Osobowych (UODO), or another competent authority. Use the contact in section 1; identity checks are proportionate and avoid unnecessary documents. We respond without undue delay, normally within one month, and communicate justified extensions under the GDPR. We usually answer in 2 working days.

Commenters/subscribers should first contact the creator as controller. IRIS forwards requests it receives to the relevant customer and assists under the DPA. An unsubscribe blocks further sending within that account; it does not automatically erase all data.

We automatically match comments to the campaign rules that the customer sets, and apply sending and sign-in limits and security controls. IRIS makes no decisions based solely on automated processing that produce legal effects concerning a person or similarly significantly affect a person (Article 22 GDPR).

The nearest to such a decision is the automatic pause of sending from a customer's account. IRIS checks the emails sent from the account in the last 30 days (test sends excluded), when there are at least 200. Sending is paused when bounces reach 1% of these emails (and at least 5) or recipient complaints reach 0.2% (and at least 3). The pause stops emails, and replies and messages on Instagram, from that account; the IRIS account, the data and the Instagram connection stay. The users of the account get an email with the reason. A person resumes sending: the Operator, after a check of the list together with the customer. The customer can give its view, contest the pause and ask for a review by a person: reply to that email or write to support@irisapp.pl. We usually answer in 2 working days.
