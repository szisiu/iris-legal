§ 1. Parties and definitions

  1. This data processing agreement (the “Agreement”) is concluded between:
    1. the customer who has an account in the IRIS service and accepts the Agreement in the Service (the “Controller”), and
    2. [OPERATOR: name or business name, legal form, register, address, NIP, REGON, telephone], the operator of the IRIS service (the “Processor”),
    together the “Parties”.
  2. In this Agreement:
    1. GDPR means Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 27 April 2016 on the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data, and repealing Directive 95/46/EC (General Data Protection Regulation);
    2. Service means the IRIS application provided by the Processor;
    3. Terms means the terms of use of the Service published at terms.html;
    4. Data means the personal data described in Annex 1, processed by the Processor on behalf of the Controller, including commenters' data before they provide an email address;
    5. Commenter means a person whose comment on the Controller's post is processed by the Service, including someone who does not reply or provide an email address;
    6. Sub-processor means another processor within the meaning of Article 28(2) and (4) GDPR;
    7. Sub-processor List means the list published at subprocessors.html;
    8. Breach means a personal data breach within the meaning of Article 4(12) GDPR.
  3. Other terms have the meaning given to them in the GDPR.
  4. The Agreement forms part of the contractual relationship between the Parties based on the Terms.

§ 2. Subject matter, nature and purpose of processing

  1. The Controller entrusts the Processor with the processing of the Data, and the Processor undertakes to process it on behalf of the Controller on the terms set out in the Agreement.
  2. Processing is automated, takes place within the Service, and comprises collecting, recording, storing, viewing, using, transmitting and erasing the Data.
  3. The purpose of processing is to provide the Service to the Controller, namely:
    1. collecting leads from comments on posts on the Controller's Instagram account;
    2. replying to commenters by private message;
    3. creating contacts for commenters, collecting email addresses, confirming and storing the Controller's subscribers;
    4. sending the Controller's emails and broadcasts;
    5. measuring email delivery and link clicks;
    6. honouring unsubscribes and erasure requests.
  4. The types of Data and the categories of data subjects are set out in Annex 1.
  5. The Service is not intended for processing special categories of personal data referred to in Article 9 GDPR, and the Controller does not use it for that purpose.
  6. The Agreement does not cover the Controller's own data as a customer of the Service, including its account and billing data and sender identity (name, postal and contact email addresses published in email footers and on capture pages). The Processor is the controller of that data, and its processing is described in the Privacy Policy.

§ 3. Duration

  1. The Agreement applies from its acceptance in the Service for as long as the Controller's account exists in the Service.
  2. The Agreement expires when the Controller's account is deleted or the contractual relationship based on the Terms ends. § 12 applies after it expires.
  3. Disconnecting Instagram, or removing IRIS in the Instagram settings, does not delete the IRIS account or end the Agreement. When Meta tells IRIS that the app was removed, IRIS marks the connection as disabled and stops sending messages from it and reading its comments. In the same operation IRIS deletes the encrypted access token of that connection from the database. Contacts and campaigns remain; reconnecting allows service to resume.

§ 4. Processing on documented instructions

  1. The Processor processes the Data only on documented instructions from the Controller, including with regard to transfers of the Data to a third country or an international organisation, unless required to do so by Union or Member State law to which the Processor is subject. In such a case, the Processor informs the Controller of that legal requirement before processing, unless that law prohibits such information on important grounds of public interest.
  2. The Controller's documented instructions are:
    1. the Agreement and the Terms;
    2. the Controller's configuration of the Service, including connecting an Instagram account, campaign settings, keywords, reply text, capture pages and email content;
    3. the Controller's actions in the Service, including starting a send, exporting data and deleting the account;
    4. instructions sent to privacy@irisapp.pl from the email address assigned to the Controller's account.
  3. The Processor immediately informs the Controller if, in its opinion, an instruction infringes the GDPR or other Union or Member State data protection provisions.
  4. The Processor does not process the Data for its own purposes; in particular it does not sell the Data, use it for advertising, or use it to train machine-learning models.

§ 5. Confidentiality

  1. The Processor allows only persons it has authorised to process the Data, to the extent necessary to perform the Agreement, who have committed themselves to confidentiality or are under an appropriate statutory obligation of confidentiality.
  2. The confidentiality obligation continues after the Agreement expires.

§ 6. Security of processing

  1. The Processor takes the measures required under Article 32 GDPR, described in Annex 2.
  2. The Processor may change those measures provided the level of protection of the Data is not reduced.

§ 7. Sub-processors

  1. The Controller gives the Processor general written authorisation to engage Sub-processors. The Sub-processors the Processor uses, and those it plans to use, are named in the Sub-processor List.
  2. The Processor informs the Controller of any intended addition or replacement of a Sub-processor at least 14 days before that Sub-processor begins processing the Data, by email to the address assigned to the Controller's account and by updating the Sub-processor List. This also applies to a Sub-processor marked on the List as planned, before it begins processing the Data.
  3. Within 14 days of receiving the notice, the Controller may object. The Parties will try to resolve the matter in good faith. If the objection is not accommodated, the Controller may terminate the Agreement, together with the relationship based on the Terms, with immediate effect by deleting the account; § 12 applies.
  4. The Processor imposes on each Sub-processor, by contract, the same data protection obligations as set out in the Agreement, in particular the obligation to provide sufficient guarantees to implement appropriate technical and organisational measures. Where a Sub-processor fails to fulfil its obligations, the Processor remains liable to the Controller for the performance of that Sub-processor's obligations.
  5. Meta Platforms Ireland Ltd. is not a Sub-processor. For data on its platform it acts as an independent controller under its own terms (Meta Platform Terms). The Processor exchanges data with it on the Controller's instructions, including receiving comments and messages and sending replies through the connected Instagram account.

§ 8. Transfers of Data outside the European Economic Area

  1. The Processor transfers Data outside the EEA only in accordance with Chapter V GDPR and on the Controller's documented instructions. Acceptance of the Agreement does not replace the required transfer basis.
  2. Data is transferred outside the EEA in two cases:
    1. Resend (Plus Five Five, Inc., USA) sends messages from the AWS eu-west-1 region (Ireland), but it stores account data, message metadata, logs and API records in the USA. Resend uses further processors in the USA, among them Amazon Web Services, Inc.;
    2. Cloudflare, Inc. (USA) handles traffic through a global network and terminates TLS, so it sees the Data passing through the Service, also outside the EEA.
    The application and the database run on an OVHcloud server in France, and the operator's mail is stored in France. Companies of the OVHcloud group can have remote access to them, among them from Canada and the United Kingdom. The Sub-processor List gives the scope, locations and transfer bases.
  3. The basis for the transfers to Resend and Cloudflare is the Standard Contractual Clauses adopted by Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2021/914, Module Three (processor to processor), included in these vendors' data processing agreements. In those agreements both vendors also state that they take part in the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework (Article 45 GDPR). Access by OVHcloud companies from Canada and the United Kingdom relies on Commission adequacy decisions (Article 45 GDPR), and access from other countries on Standard Contractual Clauses concluded within the OVHcloud group.
  4. The Controller instructs transfers within the scope of the Sub-processor List, subject to paragraphs 1 and 3. A new transfer follows § 7(2) and (3).

§ 9. Assistance with data subjects' rights

  1. Taking into account the nature of the processing, the Processor assists the Controller in fulfilling its obligation to respond to requests for exercising the data subject's rights laid down in Chapter III GDPR, in particular it:
    1. provides an account data export in the Service within the scope described in § 12(1);
    2. on the Controller's instruction, erases a named person's data from linked contact, conversation, consent, delivery and automation records and from located webhooks and mail events; invalidates ready account exports. A suppression entry remains detached from the contact as described in Annex 1. Click counts name no recipient and no send, so they remain unchanged (Annex 1). The contact-erasure function does not cover the other places that can hold the person's data. For them these rules apply:
      1. the Controller changes or deletes in the Service itself a username in campaign settings (the list of ignored accounts) and other content it wrote; on its instruction the Processor states how many campaigns hold the named username;
      2. the Service has no function to delete one uploaded image; on the Controller's instruction sent to privacy@irisapp.pl the Processor deletes the named image from the database. Cached copies of the image can remain as § 12(4) says;
      3. job logs hold identifiers only and are deleted by the rotation described in Annex 1; the Processor does not delete single entries from them;
      4. copies of emails at Resend are deleted by Resend 30 days after sending;
      5. contact deletion in the Service finds a person's data by email address and Instagram identifier, and the Processor's tool also by username. These functions can miss data written in another way, for example a first name in the text of a comment.
      The Processor tells the Controller what was deleted, what remains and why.
    3. honours unsubscribes made by recipients through the unsubscribe link in a message.
  2. If a data subject addresses a request directly to the Processor, the Processor forwards it to the Controller without delay.

§ 10. Assistance with Articles 32 to 36 GDPR

Taking into account the nature of processing and the information available to it, the Processor assists the Controller in ensuring compliance with the obligations in Articles 32 to 36 GDPR concerning security of processing, notification of Breaches to the supervisory authority, communication to data subjects, data protection impact assessments and prior consultation, in particular by providing, on request, information about the processing and the measures applied.

§ 11. Personal data breaches

  1. The Processor notifies the Controller of a Breach without undue delay, and no later than 48 hours after discovering it, by email to the address assigned to the Controller's account.
  2. The notice contains, as far as available, the information listed in Article 33(3) GDPR: the nature of the Breach, the categories and approximate number of data subjects and records concerned, the likely consequences, the measures taken or proposed, and contact details. Information not available at the time of the notice is provided without undue delay as it becomes available.
  3. The Processor documents Breaches and cooperates with the Controller in investigating them. Notification to the supervisory authority and communication to data subjects are made by the Controller.

§ 12. Return and deletion of Data at the end

  1. The Controller may at any time download an account export, including without accepting a new Agreement version. The ZIP contains ten data files: contacts, consent records, suppressions, audit events, campaigns, email templates, broadcasts, brand, billing and the content restrictions IRIS placed, plus README.txt. It does not include separate data sets of capture conversations, deliveries, broadcast recipients, clicks, automation executions, outbound intents, raw webhooks, mail events or image files. Before account deletion the Service prepares this export, available for 24 hours. The Controller chooses return of Data or deletion; after return, the Processor deletes remaining copies under the rules below. If the Controller also wants the data sets that the export does not include, it asks at privacy@irisapp.pl and deletes the account only after it receives them. The Processor supplies them in CSV or JSON files, and the images as files, within 30 days of the request. Account deletion cannot be undone and there are no backups, so after the deletion these data sets cannot be supplied. Preparing the file before account deletion does not require the Controller to download it.
  2. Account deletion starts the sequence of export, ending connections and services and purging account records. The Processor deletes Data from the database in that sequence, no later than 30 days after the Agreement expires, with these exceptions:
    1. mail events that the Service did not assign to an account remain until 90 days after receipt;
    2. the ready export remains available for 24 hours;
    3. account deletion proof remains for 12 months after completion; Meta deletion-request records remain for 12 months after receipt; a pending Meta request is not purged during handling;
    4. Data required by Union or Member State law is retained only to the extent and for the period required.
    The Service deletes the account's other mail events in that sequence, and it does not store events that arrive later and name the deleted account. The mail events in point 1 hold the recipient's address and delivery data, but they have no account key, because the event does not name the account and no delivery record in the database assigns it; so account deletion does not find them, and the deletion job removes them when that period ends. This is a known limit of the Service and not a duty to keep the data. If a step of the account deletion fails, the Service repeats it automatically and then writes the error to the log. If the deletion does not finish within 30 days, the Processor tells the Controller and completes the deletion without undue delay.
  3. The suppression list is deleted with the account. The customer's own data, including billing records and the customer record retained at Stripe, falls under the Privacy Policy rather than this processing arrangement.
  4. IRIS makes no backups of the database today, so deleted Data does not stay in backups. After account deletion these can remain outside the database:
    1. copies of public images in the caches of browsers and intermediate servers, Cloudflare included, for up to a year, as the cache header says; the Service does not clear these caches;
    2. job log entries with identifiers, until the rotation described in Annex 1 deletes them;
    3. copies of emails at Resend, for up to 30 days after sending;
    4. data in the systems of Cloudflare and OVHcloud, which delete it under their own data processing agreements.

§ 13. Information and audits

  1. The Processor makes available to the Controller all information necessary to demonstrate compliance with the obligations laid down in Article 28 GDPR, and allows for and contributes to audits, including inspections, conducted by the Controller or an auditor it mandates.
  2. An audit is conducted first by review of documentation or of the Processor's answers to the Controller's questionnaire.
  3. An on-site audit is possible on at least 14 days' notice, at the Controller's cost, no more than once a year, unless it follows a Breach.
  4. The auditor keeps confidential the information obtained in the audit.

§ 14. Rights and obligations of the Controller

  1. The Controller is responsible for the lawfulness of the processing of the Data, in particular for having a legal basis for processing, for informing data subjects (Articles 13 and 14 GDPR), for the lawfulness of its instructions and of the content of the messages sent, and for complying with Meta's rules.
  2. The Controller has the right to give instructions (§ 4), to object to a Sub-processor (§ 7), to receive Breach notices (§ 11), to require erasure of the Data (§ 9 and § 12) and to carry out audits (§ 13).

§ 15. Liability

  1. The Parties' liability under the Agreement is subject to the exclusions and limitations set out in the Terms, subject to paragraphs 2 and 3 and the obligations in § 7(4).
  2. Paragraph 1 does not limit liability that cannot be excluded or limited by law, liability for damage caused intentionally, or liability to data subjects under Article 82 GDPR.
  3. Towards a Controller who is a consumer, or a trader who has the protection given to a consumer, the limitations in paragraph 1 apply only as far as mandatory law allows.

§ 16. Changes to the Agreement

  1. The Processor may change the Agreement by publishing a new version with a new version number.
  2. The Controller accepts a new version in the Service by ticking “I accept the Terms of Service, version {number}, and the data processing agreement, version {number}” (in Polish: “Akceptuję Regulamin w wersji {numer} i umowę powierzenia w wersji {numer}”). A Controller that has not yet accepted any version can, until it accepts, use the Service only to export data and delete the account. A Controller that accepted an earlier version has a read-only account from the day the new version binds it until it accepts that version: it can view and export data, delete the account, change the plan, pause automation or a campaign, delete a campaign or a contact and stop sending to a contact, but it cannot create or change campaigns, lists, tags, templates or broadcasts. Campaigns that are running continue during that time, and the Service continues to process Data in them.
  3. Acceptance is recorded with the time, a truncated IP address and the browser's user agent.
  4. A Controller who does not accept a new version may delete the account; § 12 applies.
  5. A change to the Sub-processor List is not a change to the Agreement and follows the procedure in § 7.
  6. Number 3 identifies this document draft. Application code contains only version 1, marked is_placeholder=true, with one sentence of draft text; version 2 was never deployed in the product. Version 3 requires separate deployment, availability and acceptance in the Service. Publishing the draft is neither that deployment nor evidence of earlier version 2 acceptance.

§ 17. Final provisions

  1. The Agreement is governed by Polish law. Matters not covered by it are governed by the GDPR and the Polish Civil Code.
  2. Disputes arising from the Agreement are resolved by the Polish courts, in accordance with the Terms.
  3. The Agreement is drawn up in Polish. The English translation (this page) is for information; where the two differ, the Polish text prevails.
  4. Annexes 1 and 2 form an integral part of the Agreement.

Annex 1. Data, data subjects, purposes and retention

Categories of data subjects

  • commenters on the Controller's posts, including those who never reply or provide an email address;
  • people messaging the Controller's Instagram account;
  • people providing an email address by DM or on a capture page;
  • recipients of the Controller's messages and people clicking tracked links;
  • people whose data appears in content and images supplied by the Controller.

Types of data

  • Instagram user, comment, post and message identifiers, username, comment and incoming message text in raw webhooks;
  • outgoing DM and public-reply text, links and recipient identifier in send requests;
  • email address and its normalized form, contact and confirmation status, list and tag assignments, unsubscribe and suppression state, encrypted unsubscribe token;
  • consent record: scope, channel, time, copy key and recorded wording, language, source comment and post identifiers where available. For a DM the wording is read from the sent private reply. If no such record exists, the Service records wording generated at capture for resource delivery, without recording marketing consent. A record alone does not establish valid consent.
  • conversation state, verification attempts and method, keyed digest of the six-digit code and hash of the capture-page token; the code itself may occur in message text;
  • recipient address, delivery identifiers and events, bounce and complaint diagnostics; click counts: link, day (UTC) and number of clicks that day, without naming the recipient or the send; the link in a message carries the link identifier alone;
  • the content of campaigns, templates, broadcasts, messages, links, list and tag names, capture pages and images, to the extent it contains personal data, including Instagram usernames that the Controller puts on a campaign's list of ignored accounts;
  • contact, conversation, comment, post and message identifiers in job logs, and contact and conversation identifiers in the arguments of queued jobs. Consent records and click counts contain neither IP addresses nor browser user agents. When a person clicks a tracked link, the Service uses the person's IP address (an IPv6 address shortened to /64) and browser user agent in process memory only: the IP address for the limit of 120 counted clicks per minute from one address, and the user agent to leave out robots; it does not write them to the database or to logs. Cloudflare sees IP addresses, requests, full paths and data passing through the proxy, including forms and session cookies; § 8 and the Sub-processor List describe this flow.

The customer's account data, sender details in footers, Agreement acceptance and log of customer actions are processed by the operator as controller (§ 2(6)). Processing of special categories of data (Article 9 GDPR) is not intended.

Purposes

The purposes in § 2(3), including handling commenters before they provide an address, address verification, documenting consent, preventing further sends after an unsubscribe, and operating and diagnosing the Service.

Annex 2. Technical and organisational measures

  • Transport and proxy. HTTPS for public traffic, application HSTS for one year with subdomains outside development mode. Cloudflare terminates TLS and sees traffic content; the connection to the host passes through Cloudflare Tunnel. The connection from the cloudflared container to the application uses HTTP on the Docker network.
  • Security headers. A strict Content-Security-Policy, framing of the Service forbidden, nosniff, a referrer policy.
  • Encryption at rest. Instagram access tokens and unsubscribe tokens are encrypted with AES-256-GCM; keys can be rotated.
  • Authentication. Sign-in by single-use link; authentication tables store hashes of sign-in and session tokens. An exception is the encrypted sign-in link containing the token, retained in the email job arguments. Session cookies use HttpOnly and SameSite=Lax; CSRF protection is applied.
  • Account isolation. In the customer panel, account-data queries are scoped to the signed-in Controller's account; its identifier does not come from the request body. The separate authorized-operator panel can select an account by path identifier, with its own access controls.
  • Audit trail. Customer or operator actions are recorded within the scope provided for those actions; records are kept for 24 months, with IP truncated to /24 (IPv4) or /48 (IPv6) where recorded and without personal values in the change record. Anonymous capture, unsubscribe and confirmation operations and webhooks are not covered by a general record of every change in this audit trail.
  • Minimisation and deletion. Automatic deletion according to the periods in Annex 1; erasure of a named person's data; account export within § 12(1); account deletion with proof.
  • Webhooks. The Service accepts webhooks only with a valid signature.
  • Containers. The application, PostgreSQL and cloudflared run in Docker containers. No container publishes a port; traffic to the application passes through Cloudflare Tunnel. The application and cloudflared containers have a read-only file system and their system capabilities removed.
  • Access. Operator access is limited to the authorized operator through Cloudflare Access with a one-time PIN and a separate application session. SSH and host administration use Tailscale.
  • Outgoing mail. Resend sends messages from the eu-west-1 region with TLS enforced; open and click tracking at Resend is off.
  • External services. Cloudflare provides DNS, proxy, TLS, Tunnel, Access and caching of public images. The Service uses no external analytics, error-tracking or application log-collection service; providers' own logs are separate.
  • Location. The application and PostgreSQL run on an OVHcloud VPS in France (EEA), behind Cloudflare. OVHcloud hosts the operator's mailbox. The Sub-processor List and § 8 describe the global scope of Cloudflare and outgoing mail.

Measures the Service does not use today. IRIS makes no backups of the database today and has no procedure to restore data from backups. The Service also has no monitoring of its operation. Before backups are introduced, the Processor will add to this annex where they are kept, how they are protected and how long they are kept.
